## II. Mission

- Through power and force: popes and kings
- Through a way of life: monasticism
- Through splitting: East-West Schism

Chaos: Islamic invasion of Byzantine lands, Barbarians in the West. Roman Empire falls in 476.

- Gregory the Great (590-604) - pope as leader of Western church, missionary to the barbarians (St. Augustine of Canterbury to England, St. Boniface to Germany)
- Charlemagne (800) - crowned emperor by pope, unites much of Western Europe, promotes learning and church reform
  - He becomes emperor (holy roman emperor)
  - Pope Leo III crowns him emperor
  - While Charlemagne was praying in St. Peter's Basilica, the pope placed a crown on his head, declaring him "Emperor of the Romans." This event symbolized the fusion of Roman, Christian, and Germanic elements that characterized the Holy Roman Empire.
  - This caused tension with the Byzantine Empire, which saw itself as the continuation of the Roman Empire and viewed Charlemagne's coronation as a challenge to its authority.
  - Christianity and violence becomes intertwined in the West.
- Nicholas I (820-867) - asserts papal authority over kings
  - He is very corrupted.
  - Photian schism with Eastern church begins - he excommunicates the patriarch of Constantinople.
  - Pseudo-Isidorian decretals - forged documents that assert papal authority over secular rulers
  - The Donation of Constantine: a forged document used to justify papal authority over secular rulers, claiming that Emperor Constantine granted the pope authority over Rome and the western part of the Roman Empire.
  - The cadaver synod - Pope Stephen VI has the corpse of his predecessor, Pope Formosus, exhumed and put on trial for perjury and other crimes. The corpse is found guilty, stripped of its papal garments, and thrown into the Tiber River. This event highlights the corruption and political intrigue within the papacy during this period.
