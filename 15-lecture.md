Athanasius (4c)

Context:

- Neoplatonism
  - Hierarchy of emanations
  - Evil is the absence of good (privation theory of evil). Things are good insofar as they exist.
- Arianism
  - Son is a created being, subordinate to the Father

Story of the gospel:

- The Word who is God, called creation into existence.
- Disobedience caused humanity to fall into non-existence.
- Divine dilemma:
  - God can't let His creation return to non-existence. AND
  - God can't break His word that sin leads to death.
- Solution: The Word becomes flesh, so that in the flesh He can defeat death and restore humanity to existence.

Incarnation

- The same Word that called us into existence in the beginning attaches himself to our humanity in order to call humanity back to existence.
- The Incarnate Word defeats death, bringing life to Adam and all who believe in Him.
- Participation in the Word's humanity restores the image of God in us.

The Son of God became the Son of Man so that children of men could become children of God.

Orthodox Churches celebrate the annunciation on March 25, the same day as the vernal equinox, symbolizing new life and resurrection.

Augustine's Thought

A. Manichaeism
Good and evil as two opposing principles with equal power fighting for control over the world (the pear tree story).

His response to the manichaeans:

- original sin and the problem of evil
- human will and divine grace
- City of God vs. earthly city

City of God is not about the visible church. It is about elected people who love God and non-elect people who love the world.

For him, justification is a long process.

B. Donatism

- The church is a community of the pure. Sacraments administered by sinful priests are invalid.
  - Augustine's response:
  - Church as 'wheat and tares' instead of 'pure bride'
  - Sacraments are given by Christ, not by the priest's holiness.

C. Pelagianism

- Denies original sin and the need for divine grace.
- Perfection is achievable through human effort alone. "You are too morally tolerant."
- Emphasizes human free will and the ability to choose good without divine assistance.
  - Augustine's response:
  - Original sin affects all humanity.
  - Divine grace is essential for salvation and righteous living.
