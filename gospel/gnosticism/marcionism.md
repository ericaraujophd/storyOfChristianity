(marcionism)=
Marcionism was a significant Gnostic Christian movement founded by Marcion of Sinope (c. 85–160 CE). Marcion was a wealthy shipowner who became a prominent figure in the early Christian church. He is best known for his radical theological views, which led to his excommunication from the orthodox Christian community. He was the son of a bishop in Sinope, a city on the southern coast of the Black Sea (modern-day Turkey).

Marcion's teachings were characterized by a strong dualism, which posited a distinction between the God of the Old Testament and the God of the New Testament. He believed that the God of the Old Testament was a lesser, vengeful deity (the [Demiurge](#demiurge)) who created the material world, while the true God revealed in Jesus Christ was a higher, benevolent being who offered salvation through grace. This radical reinterpretation of Scripture led Marcion to reject the Old Testament entirely and to create his own canon of Christian texts, which included an edited version of the Gospel of Luke and ten of Paul's epistles.

Marcionism posed a significant challenge to the early church, as it forced Christian leaders to clarify their understanding of the relationship between the Old and New Testaments and to defend the unity of God's revelation in Scripture. The movement ultimately led to the development of the orthodox Christian canon and the affirmation of the Old Testament as an integral part of the Christian faith.

:::{admonition} Marcion's Community
:class: tip
After being accused of heresy and excommunicated from the orthodox Christian church around 144 CE, Marcion went on to establish his own church, which spread throughout the Roman Empire. Marcionism gained a significant following, particularly among Gentile Christians who were attracted to Marcion's emphasis on grace and his rejection of Jewish law.
:::
