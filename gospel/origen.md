title: Origen of Alexandria
subtitle: The Scholar of Scripture

(origen)=
Origen of Alexandria (c. 185–251 AD) was an early Christian theologian, scholar, and prolific writer. He is considered one of the most important figures in early Christian theology and biblical studies. Origen was born in Alexandria, Egypt, a major center of learning and culture in the ancient world. He later moved to Caesarea in Palestine, where he continued his scholarly work. He was a disciple of [Clement of Alexandria](#clement_of_alexandria) and was deeply influenced by Platonic philosophy.

Origen is best known for his extensive writings on Christian theology, biblical exegesis, and philosophy. He produced a vast body of work, including commentaries on almost every book of the Bible, theological treatises, and apologetic writings defending Christianity against pagan and heretical critiques.

(hexapla)=
Origen's Hexapla, a critical edition of the Old Testament in six parallel columns (Hebrew, Greek transliteration, and four Greek translations), was a monumental scholarly achievement that greatly influenced biblical studies.

Origen also developed the concept of the "spiritual interpretation" of Scripture, emphasizing the importance of understanding the deeper, allegorical meanings behind the literal text. This approach to biblical interpretation had a lasting impact on Christian theology and exegesis.

His ideas can be read at [New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0412.htm).

Origen's theological stands on Scripture interpretation can be controversial. His analysis of allegorical meanings sometimes led him to conclusions that were at odds with orthodox Christian doctrine. For example, he proposed the pre-existence of souls and the eventual restoration of all beings, including the devil, which were later deemed heretical by the church. Still, his contributions to Christian thought and biblical scholarship remain significant. He is considered the first great biblical scholar of the early church.

His understanding that the Eternal Word (Logos) is incarnated in the text of the Scripture is a foundational idea for Christian theology. Origen believed that the Word of God is not only revealed through Jesus Christ but also through the sacred texts of the Bible. This concept underscores the importance of Scripture as a means of encountering and understanding God's revelation.

Also, some principles of Origen's hermeneutics (interpretation of Scripture) include:

1. Only those who believe that all Scripture is inspired by the Holy Spirit are able to interpret Scripture correctly.
2. An interpretation must be edifying to God's people.
3. The Holy Spirit accommodates Scripture to the different capacities of people so that Scripture speaks to everyone. Although many people are only able to understand the literal meaning of Scripture, the Spirit both "edifies" them and "raises their apprehension" to a spiritual level through various means such as stumbling blocks.
4. Every part of Scripture is profitable.
5. The character of the interpreter is important. An interpreter should devote one's whole soul to the words of God, be attentive, careful, diligent, reverent, of sound judgment, etc. The meaning of a text is hidden in proportion to the unworthiness of the interpreter.

Origen was martyred during the persecution of Christians under the Roman Emperor Decius around 251 AD. Despite the controversies surrounding some of his theological ideas, Origen's legacy as a pioneering scholar and theologian continues to be recognized in Christian history.

Origen reportedly castrated himself as a young man to avoid any temptation of lust, following a literal interpretation of Matthew 19:12. This extreme act reflects his intense commitment to asceticism and spiritual purity, although it was not a common practice among early Christians.

:::{admonition} 🤪 Fun Facts 2
:icon: false
Origen lost his dad as a martyr when he was just a child. His father, Leonides, was arrested and executed for his Christian faith during the persecution under Emperor Septimius Severus around 202 AD. Trying to follow his father's example, Origen sought to be martyred himself at a young age. However, his mother hid all his clothes to prevent him from going out and seeking martyrdom, leaving him ashamed to go out naked.
