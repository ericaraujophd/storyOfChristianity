(creeds)=
The creeds are concise statements of Christian belief that summarize the essential doctrines of the faith. They serve as a "rule of faith" that helps to define orthodox Christian beliefs and distinguish them from heretical teachings, such as those found in Gnosticism.

The main purposes of the creeds are to:

1. organize teaching for catechumens
2. express identity at baptism
3. unify the church in all times and places
4. express communal faith in worship
5. guide reading of Scripture
6. fight heresy

The most well-known creeds in Christian history are the Apostles' Creed and the Nicene Creed. The Apostles' Creed is an early statement of Christian belief that likely developed from the baptismal confession used in the early church. It summarizes key beliefs about God the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit.

## 📜 The Apostles' Creed

The Apostles' Creed is one of the oldest and most widely used creeds in Christianity. It is traditionally attributed to the apostles, although it was likely developed over time by early Christian communities. The creed affirms essential Christian doctrines, including the nature of God, the incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus Christ, as well as beliefs about the Holy Spirit, the church, and eternal life.

The Apostles Creed (ca. 1550–1600). Each of the fifteen compartments in this tapestry contains a single scene representing an article of the Apostles’ Creed, a set of declarations of Christian belief dating back to the fourth century. One of few surviving tapestries to depict this subject, this piece is unique for its inclusion of every article in a single hanging, without any accompanying inscriptions. The straightforward, even provincial design of the compositions makes them easily legible, unfolding like a comic strip from top to bottom. The series opens with God creating heaven and earth and ends with an image of everlasting life. ## 📜 The Nicene Creed

The Nicene Creed was formulated at the First Council of Nicaea in 325 AD and later expanded at the First Council of Constantinople in 381 AD. It was created to address various heresies, particularly Arianism, which denied the full divinity of Jesus Christ. The Nicene Creed affirms the belief in the Trinity and the full divinity and humanity of Jesus Christ.

## Three ways to frame the creeds to our contemporary churches

---

1.

Jesus is the most amazing, confounding, alarming, and captivating person ever to walk the planet. Even as the early church loved and worshiped Jesus, they found describing him beyond available words. Over time they assembled carefully formed truths about Jesus into creeds.  Creeds were one way to clearly describe and confess their faith in this most amazing person ever to live, and a way to invite novices into deeper understanding about who he is. We invite you to say a creed now as a way to enter into the mystery of Jesus.

---

2.

In the early church, spiritual novices memorized and spoke creeds as a way to take in the faith and to let it grow deep inside.  At their baptism, new believers would say the creeds as their first statement of faith.  Veteran Christians would say it together to reinforce their own belief.  In that spirit, whether you’re a spiritual novice or a veteran, we invite you to say these ancient and time-tested words.  

---

3.

The church is not a place where we make up what we believe.  It is a place where we receive the true story of the world.  In the New Testament, the Apostle Paul talks about this faithful handing down of the gospel.  Let’s use the words of the Apostles’ Creed to receive the story that has been handed down.
