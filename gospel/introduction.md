As the church growed and spread throughout the Roman Empire, they faced many challenges. Some of them were acknowledged such as persecution and misunderstandings from the surrounding culture. Other challenges came from within the Christian community itself, as different interpretations of Jesus' life and teachings emerged. But one of the most significant challenges came from a group known as the Gnostics.

Let's remember that the context where the early church lived was one of polytheism, dualism, syncretism, neo-platonism, and mystery religions. In this context, the early Christians had to articulate what they believed about Jesus Christ and his significance for salvation. Gnosticism, with its emphasis on secret knowledge (gnosis) and its dualistic worldview, posed a significant challenge to the early church's understanding of the Gospel.

In order to defend and articulate the true Gospel message, the early church distinguished itself from Gnostic beliefs and the culture of their time by relying on these four pillars:

- Creed (the story, the narrative that makes sense of Scripture)
- Canon (the authoritative books that make up Scripture)
- Holy Spirit (the guide and teacher of the church)
- Leaders (the teachers and pastors who help interpret and apply Scripture)

This part of the course starts with an introduction to Gnosticism, followed by an exploration of two major Gnostic movements: Marcionism and Docetism. Finally, we will examine how the early church responded to these challenges through the development of the canon, creeds, and apostolic succession.
