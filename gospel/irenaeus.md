(irenaeus)=
Irenaeus of Lyons (c. 130–202 AD) was an early Christian theologian and bishop known for his role in combating heresies, particularly Gnosticism. Born in Asia Minor, he later became the bishop of Lyons in Gaul (modern-day France). Irenaeus is best known for his work "Against Heresies" (Adversus Haereses), in which he systematically refuted Gnostic teachings and defended orthodox Christian doctrine.

## Life and Background

Irenaeus was likely born in Smyrna (modern-day Izmir, Turkey) and was a student of Polycarp, who was himself a disciple of the Apostle John. This connection to the apostolic tradition gave Irenaeus a strong foundation in early Christian teachings. He later moved to Gaul, where he became the bishop of Lyons around 177 AD.

## Theological Contributions

Irenaeus is possibly one of the biggest contributors to the development of Christian theology in the 2nd century. He is also known as the Theologian of Unities because of his emphasis on the unity of God, Christ, and the Church. These unities include:

- Unity of God, as creator and redeemer
- Unity of the person of Jesus, as Word and flesh
- Unity of human history, as the working-out of a single, purposeful divine plan
- Unity of revelation, both the OT Scriptures and the documents of the new covenant
- Unity of the Christian faith throughout the world
- Unity and continuity of the Church
- Unity of the human being (body and soul), also in the resurrection

His stand on these unities was a direct response to Gnostic dualism, which posited a sharp divide between the material and spiritual worlds.

Irenaeus is also a pioneeer on developing and emphasizing the importance for the church to have The "Rule of Faith/Truth". According to Irenaeus, the Rule of Faith is a summary of apostolic teaching that serves as a guide for interpreting Scripture and maintaining orthodox belief. This Rule of Faith includes several key elements:

- This story guides right exegesis
- This story is handed on by way of tradition
- This story unifies the church

Therefore, the Gnostic reading of Scripture is invalid because it is not congruent with the Rule of Faith. In other words, Gnostics don't have the right story guiding their reading of Scripture.

## The Gospel According to Irenaeus

According to Irenaus, the Gospel is a seamlessly unified story of God forming humanity into the **image of Christ**, bringing us **from infancy to maturity**.

The unified story can be seen while the two hands of God, Word and Spirit, are forming humanity into image of God (from Gen. 1 until the consummation).

For Irenaeus, Humanity is originally good, free, and has capacity for growth. That means that the first humans were created innocent like toddlers and rebellious like teens. In this sense, the Old Testament is a period of education and growth, where God disciplines humanity like a loving parent. The New Testament is the period of maturity, where humanity is formed into the image of Christ through the incarnation, death, and resurrection of Jesus.

The Incarnation, then, sums up God’s plan ("recapitulates the divine economy"), i.e., Christ sums up or brings to completion the process of bringing humanity from infancy to maturity, from innocence to experience.

In Christ, God's creation intent is fully revealed. We see the mature image of God, the destiny of all those who grow into the likeness of Christ through the work of the Spirit. "Christ became what we are that He might bring us to be even what He is," i.e., the mature Adam, the image and likeness of God, and fully united to God.

Union or communion with God is fully restored in the consummation, when God’s intent at creation (Gen. 1:26) is fully realized.

This unified story contrasts with the modern Western tendency to divide human life into sacred and secular spheres. And also contrasts with the Creation-Fall-Redemption framework that sees human history as a tragic fall from grace that needs to be redeemed. It presents a more holistic and hopeful view of human history as a journey of growth and maturation under the guidance of God.
