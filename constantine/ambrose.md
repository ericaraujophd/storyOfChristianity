(ambrose)=
Ambrose of Milan (c. 340-397 A.D.) was a Roman governor who became the Bishop of Milan and one of the most influential church leaders of the 4th century. Known for his eloquence, theological insight, and strong leadership, Ambrose played a crucial role in shaping the early Christian Church.

The Emperor Theodosius is forbidden by Saint Ambrose to enter Milan Cathedral. Anthony van Dyck, 1635-1640. [Source](https://www.nationalgalleryimages.co.uk/asset/1341/)
:::

While governor, Ambrose was unexpectedly elected bishop by popular acclaim, despite being a catechumen (a person preparing for baptism). He embraced his new role with dedication and zeal. He went to the church in order to help calming down Arians and ortodox who wanted the next bishop to be one of their own. After a child cried out "Ambrose, bishop!" the people took it as a divine sign and tried to electe him on the spot. Ambrose failed successfully in his attempts to flee from the city. After the emperor showed his approval, Ambrose accepted the position and was baptized, ordained, and consecrated as bishop within a week.

Ambrose sought to devote himself fully to his episcopal duties. He was known for his strong stance against Arianism, popularizing the works of the Cappadocian Fathers in the West, and his efforts to promote Nicene Christianity.

> He became a small babe so that you could be fully grown, perfect human beings; he was wrapped in swaddling clothes so that you might be freed from the bonds of death; he came to the manger to bring you to the altar; he was on earth so you might be in heaven. *Commentary on Luke 2.41*

After the invasion of Rome by the Goths in 410, Ambrose requested that the golden vessels of the church would be melted down to pay the ransom for the captives taken by the invaders. The criticism he received-mainly by the Arians' party-for this act of charity did not deter him. He famously responded that the church's treasures were meant to help people, not to adorn buildings.

Ambrose had a significant influence on [Augustine of Hippo](#augustine), one of the most important theologians in Christian history. Ambrose's preaching and writings helped shape Augustine's understanding of Christian doctrine and spirituality. In 387 A.D., Ambrose baptized Augustine in Milan, marking a pivotal moment in Augustine's spiritual journey.

But the most famous event of Ambrose's episcopate was his confrontation with Emperor Theodosius I after the massacre of Thessalonica in 390 A.D. One source tells that a Roman general was lynched in an urban riot after arresting a popular charioteer. In retaliation, Theodosius ordered his troops to massacre thousands of citizens in Thessalonica. When Theodosius came to Milan, Ambrose refused to allow him to enter the church until he did public penance for his actions. This bold stance by Ambrose demonstrated the growing power and influence of the Church in relation to the state.
