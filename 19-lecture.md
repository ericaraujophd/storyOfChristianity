## Dominic (d. 1221) and the Dominicans

Dominic de Guzmán was a Spanish priest and founder of the Dominican Order (Order of Preachers). Born around 1170 in Caleruega, Spain, Dominic was deeply committed to combating heresy and promoting orthodox Christian teachings. He believed that education and preaching were essential tools for addressing the spiritual needs of the time.

He founder the 'Friars Preachers' (Dominicans) in 1216, with a focus on preaching, teaching, and scholarly pursuits. The Dominicans played a significant role in the intellectual life of the medieval church, establishing schools and universities across Europe.

These friars became a shock force intellectuals (the Inquisition would later be associated with them) to defend the faith against heresies like the Albigensians.

### Definition of scholaticism

The rational attempt to analyze the revealed data of faith through a logical apparatus.

Scripture becomes less of a story and more of a source of propositional truths to be analyzed logically.

### The Dialect Method (from Aristotle)

A method of reasoning.

1. Question
2. Objections: provide answers by earlier Christian authorities who seem to hold a different view.
3. On the contrary: provide a supporting argument for your view. Arguments for and against these anwers are provide.
4. I answer that: your own answer to the question, with supporting arguments.

### The constructions of cathedrals

The cathedrals were built to reflect the glory of God. They were designed to inspire awe and reverence, with their soaring spires, intricate stained glass windows, and elaborate sculptures. The architecture of the cathedrals was meant to reflect the order and harmony of God's creation, and to provide a space for worship and contemplation.

### Bernard of Clairvaux (1090-1153)

A Cistercian monk who opposed the rise of scholasticism. He emphasized faith over reason and was wary of the intellectualization of theology. Bernard believed that the mysteries of faith could not be fully comprehended through human reason alone, and he stressed the importance of personal devotion and mystical experience in the Christian life.

His theology is categorized as Augustinian and mystical, with an emphasis on union with Christ.

He is calling people back to simplicity and devotion. Deep piety. Contemplation of Christ experience of divine love.

He attacked bad forms of scholasticism that were too rationalistic. E.g. Abelard's Sic et Non (Yes and No).

Abelard would follow the scholastic method but never give a warrantied answer. Always questioning. That would drive Abelard mad.
