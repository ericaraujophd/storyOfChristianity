## Council of Nicaea (325)

Most of the bishops were from the East, only 6 from the West. But a global representation.

The west was suffering with barbarian invasions, so the East was more stable.

Athanasius attended but did not vote.

One -> Nous -> Soul -> World

## Christological Controversy (5c)

Greek philosophy required an intermediary between God and the world. This intermediary couldn't be God nor man, according to the Greeks. The church then presents a counter-cultural narrative: Jesus is fully God and fully man.

Not human (docetism)

1 person w/ 1 divine soul
1 person w/ 1 divine spirit
2 persons w/ 2 natures (Nestorius)
1 person w/ 2 natures (Chalcedon)
(check all these terms later)

theotokos: Mary gave birth to God (according to the flesh)

Nestorius: rejected theotokos, preferred christotokos. Mary gave birth to the human Jesus, not to the divine Logos. Analogy to MIB (Men in Black) where the alien is inside the human.

The council of calcedon (451) will define the 4 rules of Christology:

1. Jesus is one person
2. Jesus has two natures
3. The two natures are unconfused
4. The two natures are indivisible

The Chalcedonian definition was accepted by both East and West, but some churches rejected it (monophysites).

Monophysites: Jesus has only one nature (divine). The human nature is absorbed by the divine nature. (Eutyches)
Monothelites: Jesus has two natures but only one will (divine). The human will is absorbed by the divine will.

The Christological controversies were not just theological debates but had significant political and social implications. Different regions and churches aligned themselves with different Christological positions, leading to schisms and the formation of separate churches.

This is the moment when real schisms start to happen in the church.
