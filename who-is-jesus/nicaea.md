title: The Council of Nicaea (325 A.D.)
subtitle: Homoousious and the Trinity

(nicaea)=
The Council of Nicaea, held in 325 A.D., was the first ecumenical council of the Christian Church. It was convened by Emperor Constantine I in the city of Nicaea (modern-day İznik, Turkey) to address the Arian controversy and to establish a unified Christian doctrine. The council brought together bishops from across the Roman Empire to discuss and resolve theological disputes that were threatening the unity of the church.

The First Council of Nicaea. Icon, 14th century.
:::

Most of the bishops who attended the Council of Nicaea were from the East. Only 6 of them came from the West. Still, there was a global representation in the room.

The low attendance from the West can be attributed to the fact that the Western Church was less centralized and faced more immediate threats from barbarian invasions, which made travel and participation more difficult.

Some of the important figures present at this councul include [Athanasius](#athanasius), who attended but did not vote.

The discussion revolved around Arius arguments that there was a time when the Son was not (a created being). For him, the Son of God was created, and then adopted into divinity. Arius was concerned to protect God's attributes (one, eternal, unchangeable, transcendent), so he draws a line in the Greek hierarchy of being and places Son on the creature side of line.

The Council will respond to that saying that the Son is *homoousious* (of the same substance) with the Father. 

The neoplatonic equation that Arius used to understand the nature of Jesus was the idea of the structure of **One -> Nous -> Soul -> World**. That was a common way of thinking in the Hellenistic worldview. All things emanate from the **One** and all things will return to the **One**. The **Nous** and **Soul** emanate from the **One**.  The **Soul** generates the world. Things are good insofar as they exist. Evil is the absence of good.

The gospel narrative of the time would be that the Bible is about salvation. That is a starting point (in contrast with Greek philosophy).

The fact is that, even after the Council of Nicaea had decided the way to understand the nature of Christ, debates continued. Between 325 and 381 A.D., the world was thrown in a discussion about *homoousious* and *homoiousios*. Reports tell us that even the laypeople were debating these terms in the marketplaces.

According to Gregory of Nyssa,

> Every part of the city is filled with such talk: the alleys, the crossroads, the squares, the avenues. It comes from those who sell clothes, money changers, grocers. If you ask a money changer what the exchange rate is, he will reply with a dissertation on the begotten and the unbegotten. If you enquire about the quality and the price of bread, the baker will reply: ‘The Father is greatest and the Son is subject to him.’ When you ask at the baths whether the water is ready, the manager will declare that the Son came forth from nothing. I do not know what name to give to this evil, whether frenzy or madness... [*On the Divinity of the Son and the Holy Spirit*, V]

[Athanasius](#athanasius) spent a great deal of his life defending the Nicene Creed. Later he would receive the reinforcement of the Cappadocian Fathers: [Basil the Great](#basil), [Gregory of Nazianzus](#gregory_of_nazianzus), and [Gregory of Nyssa](#gregory_of_nyssa). This new party will help to form the idea that God is 3 hypostases (distinctiveness of consciousness of each person of the Trinity. Another term for "person") in one *ousia* (essence or substance).

One of his main arguments was that the Word, who is God, called creation into existence. Disobedience causes humanity to fall back toward non-existence. He will also present the divine dilemma:

- God can’t let His creation return to non-existence **AND**
- God can’t break His word that sin leads to death.

Therefore, the same Word that called us into existence in the beginning attaches himself to our humanity in order to call humanity back into existence. The Incarnate Word defeats death, bringing life to Adam and all who believe. The participation in the Word's humanity restores the image of God in us.

> The Son of God became the Son of Man so that children of men could become children of God.

(pneumatomachians)=
Also, during this period, the Macedonians denied the divinity of the Holy Spirit. They were also known as pneumatomachians (fighters against the Spirit).

That is to say that there was a lot going on in the 4th century regarding the understanding of the Trinity.
