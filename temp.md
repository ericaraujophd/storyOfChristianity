Gospel stories through history:

Irenaeus in a Gnostic context:  
a seamlessly unified story of forming humanity into the image of Christ, bringing us from infancy to maturity
Athanasius in Arian and neo-Platonic context:  	
the Word calls us back into existence by uniting to humanity and defeating death
Augustine in a Manichean and Pelagian context: 
ordered loves – fallen will choosing poorly – initial gift of faith becomes active in love
Monks in a context of chaos and barbarian pagans: 
the Word gives practices through which we are conformed to Christ for mission  
Anselm in a feudal culture:  substitutionary atonement
Aquinas in a scholastic culture and church that dominates all of life:   
    church dispensing Christ’s grace through the sacraments
Reformers:  justification by grace through faith; penal substitutionary theory of the atonement   
Western Christianity today:  penalty paid in a law court 
Eastern Christianity today:  healing in a hospital
